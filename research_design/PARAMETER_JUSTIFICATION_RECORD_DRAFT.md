**Discussion.** Neighbouring bases are nearly identical templates, especially for PAC (steps of about 0.3% of the pace); model behaviour on them may be strongly correlated, so the **effective number of independent clusters may be substantially lower than `L`** (§2.3). **No effective sample size is computed or asserted**; none may be estimated from pilot behaviour. The nominal half-width 0.1922 at `L = 26` is a worst-case planning figure under independent clusters and is optimistic with respect to base similarity. Adjacent bases form couples with complementary class windows (§9.2); the cluster bootstrap resamples single bases and does not preserve couples (§12.3). The pilot bases lie close to the main bases (S 455 vs 450; G 340 vs 335); the specification requires disjointness and that no main-registry template be exposed, which holds by construction; the closeness is a recorded characteristic.

## 6. Rounding-coverage tier (§6.1)

`Xprev_max = RAMP^{N−2}(S_max)` = 505 tenths of km for the main list (`S_max = 450`; `γ = 0.04`) and 522 for the pilot list. Both are ≤ 1000, so the MIL percentage readings by floor, ceiling and round-to-nearest at 0.1 pp are **covered**. Whole-percent readings, a whole-km allowed increase and composite roundings are **not covered**. For PAC the finish-time readings of the reading set (§6.1) are covered by Proposition GP; a reader who rounds `D_dist`, converts `m:ss` to decimal minutes or rounds the pace token is not covered.
